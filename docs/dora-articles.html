DORA Art. 8
automatedclassification manual

Identification

Identify, classify and adequately document all ICT-supported business functions, roles and responsibilities, the information assets and ICT assets supporting them, and their dependencies. Keep the inventories, review them at least yearly and after every major change. (Art. 8(1), (4), (6))

What it means for this platform

The inventory already exists as code, and that is the point. Each entity's knowledge graph is the record of its information assets: every source, model, column, metric, exposure and the lineage between them, rebuilt by pf kg build and checked current in CI by pf kg check --strict. A dbt model that lands without its graph rebuilt turns the pull request red, so the inventory cannot quietly age. The ICT assets are the entity's warehouse, its pipelines, the artefact store bucket and whatever cloud account hosts the control plane.

Automated evidence

  • PASSdora-8-graph — groups/jaffle/projects/jaffle-shop/kg/graph.json present and current
  • UNVERIFIEDdora-8-asset-inventory — Prowler inventory and configuration-recording checks (config_recorder…, resourceexplorer2_… on AWS; cloudresourcemanager_… on GCP); unverified until a cloud credential is configured
  • PASSr2_bucket_inventoried — one finding per R2 bucket from pf dora r2, the artefact store's inventory

What stays a person's

  • Classifying business functions by criticality: which marts feed regulatory or financial reporting and which are analytical convenience. The graph knows the lineage, not the consequence.
  • Naming an owner per entity (dora.owner in governance/aidf.yaml, Art. 5) so the inventory has someone accountable for it.

Example

A new mart fct_settlements is added to acme-eu without rebuilding the graph. The project's kg-current CI job fails, the architecture map is stale, and dora-8-graph stays green only because the merge is blocked first. The article is satisfied by the gate, not by a yearly review.

Protection and prevention

Continuously monitor and control the security of ICT systems; minimise the impact of ICT risk through appropriate tools, policies and procedures. Ensure security of the means of transfer of data, minimise the risk of corruption, loss or unauthorised access, and implement policies limiting physical and logical access to what is needed for legitimate functions — strong authentication, encryption, network segmentation, secure configuration. (Art. 9(1)–(4))

What it means for this platform

Three surfaces carry data here, and each has an access boundary that a check can read. The repository: gate.yaml denies credential files, warehouses and the provenance record to every agent in every session, and a project session cannot write shared infrastructure. The artefact store: a bucket whose managed public domain must stay off, whose CORS must not admit any origin, and whose custom domains must enforce TLS 1.2 or newer. The organisation: members with multi-factor authentication, default repository permissions kept strict, repository creation and deletion limited. When a cloud account hosts the control plane, its IAM, KMS and network posture joins through Prowler with the DORA framework's own stamps.

Automated evidence

  • PASSdora-9-secrets-gate — gate.yaml denylist covers .env*, **/secrets.toml, **/*_key*, provenance/**
  • FAILdora-9-iam ← r2_bucket_public_access_disabled — the r2.dev domain on a bucket is enabled: every object is world-readable
  • PASSdora-9-iam ← organization_members_mfa_required — the GitHub organisation requires MFA
  • PASSdora-9-network ← zone_ssl_strict, zone_min_tls_version_secure, zone_waf_enabled — the Cloudflare zones
  • UNVERIFIEDdora-9-iam / dora-9-network — AWS or GCP IAM, KMS, security groups; unverified until that account's credential is configured

What stays a person's

  • The written access-control policy the checks enforce fragments of: who may hold the R2 token, who is an organisation owner, how a leaver is offboarded (pf offboard exists for the platform side).
  • Scoping the R2 API token to one bucket and read-only where possible; a check can see the bucket's posture, not the token's blast radius.

Example

The nightly pf dora r2 finds the scratch bucket with its r2.dev domain enabled. The finding carries status_code: FAIL, severity: High, check_id: r2_bucket_public_access_disabled. The audit places it on Article 9 by its r2_ prefix, dora-9-iam reads FAIL, every entity that lists cloudflare under dora.extra_providers shows it, and the run goes red. Disabling the managed domain in the Cloudflare dashboard turns it green the next night; nothing in the repository changes.

Detection

Have mechanisms to promptly detect anomalous activities, including ICT network performance issues and ICT-related incidents, and to identify potential material single points of failure. Detection mechanisms enable multiple layers of control, define alert thresholds and criteria to trigger response, and have sufficient resources to monitor user activity and ICT anomalies. (Art. 10(1)–(3))

What it means for this platform

Detection here is the loops. freshness-triage watches every source for staleness and volume anomalies on a two-hour cadence, pii-audit looks for personal data reaching a mart unmasked, impact-sentinel computes the blast radius of uncommitted changes, dashboard-coverage finds pages naming metrics that do not exist. Each has a token budget, a circuit breaker that opens after three consecutive failures, and a ledger, and its observations are filed as issues that close on the next clean run. For a cloud account, Prowler's logging and threat-detection checks join: CloudTrail and GuardDuty, Cloud Logging and Security Command Center, Cloudflare's bot fight mode and browser integrity check.

Automated evidence

  • PASSdora-10-loops — LOOP.md declares the monitors, their cadence and the breaker; loop-observations.yml files what they find
  • PASSdora-10-logging ← zone_bot_fight_mode_enabled, zone_browser_integrity_check_enabled — anomaly detection at the edge
  • UNVERIFIEDdora-10-logging — cloudtrail_multi_region_enabled, guardduty_is_enabled, logging_sink_created; unverified until a cloud credential is configured

What stays a person's

  • Alert thresholds are declared per loop and per group (groups/<g>/loops.yaml) and every waiver needs a reason. Deciding what a threshold should be is the business's call.
  • Watching the watcher: the breaker latches after three failures and a person clears it with a reason. An open breaker nobody reads is a detection gap this page cannot close.

Example

Monday morning, freshness-triage reports futures_prices stale on commodity-india. The group's CLAUDE.md says futures do not settle at weekends, the loop's memory carries that rule, and the finding is annotated rather than raised. The same loop, the same table, on a Wednesday: an issue labelled loop-observation opens within the run, and closes itself when the feed catches up. Detection and the threshold are both written down.

Response and recovery

Put in place a comprehensive ICT business continuity policy, with response and recovery plans that ensure continuity of critical functions, respond to and resolve ICT-related incidents so as to limit damage, activate containment measures, and estimate impacts. Test the plans, run post-incident reviews, and keep crisis communication in place. (Art. 11(1)–(7))

What it means for this platform

Containment for an autonomous system means stopping the agents. pf provenance revoke is the kill switch: it is honoured before any INTENT record is written, regardless of enforcement flags, and reinstating it is a recorded action. The governance engine's circuit breaker is the per-entity version. Continuity of the data itself rests on the warehouses being rebuildable from raw with pf seed and a dbt build, the artefact store holding the reviewed baselines outside the checkout, and, for a cloud account, backups and deletion protection that Prowler can read. Cloudflare's Always Online serving cached pages when an origin is down is the edge's small contribution.

Automated evidence

  • PASSdora-11-kill-switch — revocation armed, honoured before INTENT; the attack tests in test_provenance.py prove it
  • UNVERIFIEDdora-11-backups — backup_plans_exist, rds_instance_backup_enabled, cloudsql_instance_automated_backups; unverified until a cloud credential is configured

What stays a person's

  • The continuity policy itself: recovery time and recovery point objectives per entity, who declares an incident, who speaks to counterparties (groups/<g>/notify.yaml holds the channel, Art. 14; the plan is prose).
  • Testing recovery: rebuilding an entity from raw on a clean machine, on a schedule, with the timing recorded. A rebuild that has never been rehearsed is a plan, not a capability.

Example

A loop starts proposing metrics that reference columns no mart has. Three rejections trip the entity's breaker (CIRCUIT_BROKEN in the chain); if the pattern spreads, pf provenance revoke --reason "runaway proposals" stops every agent on the platform, and every attempt after it is a recorded refusal. The post-incident review reads the chain, not a log someone remembered to keep.

Backup policies, restoration and recovery

Develop and document backup policies and procedures specifying the scope of the data subject to backup and the minimum frequency, and restoration and recovery procedures. Backup systems that ensure integrity and are logically and physically segregated from the source; restoration tested periodically. (Art. 12(1)–(3))

What it means for this platform

Two things are worth protecting against loss or alteration here, and they are protected differently. The record of what agents did is a SHA-256 hash chain whose head is anchored to an RFC 3161 timestamp authority and OpenTimestamps: altering any record breaks every link after it, and the anchor proves when the head existed. That is integrity, which Article 12 asks of backups explicitly. The data is rebuildable from source, so its backup policy is the raw landing zone plus the artefact store, and the retention of that store is what the R2 lifecycle check reads. A bucket with no lifecycle rule has an undeclared retention.

Automated evidence

  • PASSdora-12-chain — every record hash-linked from genesis, every action with all three stages
  • FAILdora-12-anchor — never anchored; pf provenance anchor where the ledger lives fixes it
  • PASSdora-12-r2-retention ← r2_bucket_lifecycle_configured — the artefact bucket declares its retention

What stays a person's

  • Writing the scope and frequency down: what raw data is retained where, for how long, and that pf provenance export archives are taken on a cadence.
  • Restoring on purpose: rebuild an entity's warehouse and compare row counts against the last export. The check can prove the chain; only a rehearsal proves the restore.

Example

A record in provenance/chain.jsonl is edited by hand to remove a rejected metric proposal. pf provenance verify reports the hash break at that sequence and every later record as unverifiable against the last anchor; dora-12-chain reads FAIL and the nightly run is red. The regulation's "integrity of backups" is met by making tampering evident, not by asking people not to.

RTS (EU) 2024/1774 Art. 16
automated

ICT systems acquisition, development and maintenance

Adopt a policy governing the acquisition, development and maintenance of ICT systems: security requirements identified before development, source code tested and reviewed, environments separated so that changes are tested before production, source code integrity protected, and vulnerabilities in third-party components handled. (RTS Art. 16(1)–(4))

How this differs from Article 9

Article 9 protects the assets and who may reach them. RTS Article 16 governs how the software that handles them is changed. A repository can have perfect access control and still merge untested SQL straight to production; this article is about that path.

What it means for this platform

Every change here passes the same path. A commit meets the pre-commit gate: the path policy, the twelve-file cap, and the rule that a new platform module lands with a test in the same commit. A pull request meets CI: the platform suite, the graph currency check, the impact gate for anything a model depends on, the provenance chain's attack tests, and now the governance engine's own. Environments are separated by dbt target: dev, ci and base are DuckDB for every project and a production adapter loads only when selected. Third-party code is pinned under vendor/ and verified, and the dependency tree is scanned nightly against a patch window. What the repository cannot see about itself, Prowler's github provider reads from the organisation: whether the default branch is protected, reviews are required, commits are signed, secrets and dependencies are scanned, Actions permissions are read-only by default.

Automated evidence

  • PASSrts-16-commit-gate — platform/hooks/pre_commit.sh runs pf gate on every commit
  • PASSrts-16-runtime-governance — ai-governance.yml runs the engine's attack tests and pf govern check
  • FAILrts-16-github-posture ← repository_default_branch_requires_signed_commits — signed commits not required on data-platform
  • PASSrts-16-github-posture ← repository_default_branch_status_checks_required, repository_secret_scanning_enabled
  • UNVERIFIEDrts-16-branch-protection — the branch-protection API, read only with --live and a token; the nightly job passes both

What stays a person's

  • Deciding the policy the checks enforce: how many approvals a change to a regulatory mart needs, whether signed commits become mandatory, who may bypass protection (nobody, ideally: repository_default_branch_protection_applies_to_admins reads that).
  • Security requirements before development: the elevated paths and roles in each entity's governance/aidf.yaml are where "this mart needs a person" is declared.

Example

The nightly GitHub scan reports repository_default_branch_requires_multiple_approvals FAIL on Atomz-org/data-platform: one approval suffices on main. The finding lands on rts-16-github-posture, the matrix names the repository, and the fix is a branch-protection setting, not a commit. The next night the row reads PASS and the evidence, with the date it changed, is in the archived matrix and the provenance chain that recorded the audit.

How a finding becomes an article status

Every scan writes OCSF Detection Findings, Prowler's and the platform's own alike. The audit reads three things from each: its status, its severity, and how it maps to an article. A finding a scan ran with --compliance dora_2022_2554 arrives stamped with the requirement ids it evidences, and that stamp is authoritative. A finding without a stamp, from GitHub or from pf dora r2, is placed by its check-id prefix. Then the rule is simple: any failing finding at or above the entity's severity threshold fails the check; findings exercised with none failing pass it; a check with no findings at all stays unverified.

{
  "status_code": "FAIL",  "severity": "High",
  "cloud": {"provider": "cloudflare"},
  "resources": [{"uid": "r2://<account>/scratch", "type": "r2_bucket"}],
  "unmapped": {"check_id": "r2_bucket_public_access_disabled", "compliance": {}}
}
   │  r2_ prefix → dora-9-iam (Art. 9, protection and prevention)
   ▼
| Art. 9 Protection and prevention | … | **FAIL** | FAIL `dora-9-iam` — 1 failing check(s) ≥ HIGH: r2_bucket_public_access_disabled |

Each entity's resolved governance/aidf.yaml decides the threshold, which providers apply, and which articles it has declared out of scope with a reason and an owner. The matrix is written to governance/dora/matrix.md per entity, archived by the nightly run for four hundred days, and recorded to the provenance chain with its SHA-256, so a report and the chain cite each other.
